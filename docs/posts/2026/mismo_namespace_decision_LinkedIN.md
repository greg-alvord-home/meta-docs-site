One quiet reason MISMO succeeded where many software efforts struggled has nothing to do 
with performance or data volume.

Imagined a world where cars did not have a VIN (Vehicle Identification Number). You would have no way
to know anything about the vehicle's history. Every part number of every part of your car is linked to 
a single VIN. The VIN 'owns' every part of the car.

A VIN isn’t just a string; its structure and meaning are enforced.
Namespaces play a similar role in data.

MISMO adopted formats with endemic namespaces—where meaning is built into the data model itself, 
not encoded as strings and conventions.

Formats without endemic namespaces don’t eliminate complexity; they relocate it.

They push the cost of meaning onto every project, every integration, every rewrite.

Real estate finance avoided a class of reinvention costs that much of the broader software industry 
has paid repeatedly—often without realizing why.

This isn’t about nostalgia or “XML vs JSON.”
It’s about whether identity and meaning are first class, or forever re-explained at a continuing cost.
